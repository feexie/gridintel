# GEH Brief 15: "Meter every customer" (summary)

The Founder's summary of the organisers' brief. The brief itself is not in
this repository.

**Entry.** Track 3, Business Models & Financing. Setting A, Residential.
Feature freeze Sunday 1 November 2026, 01:00 WAT. Hard deadline Sunday 1

## Framing

"A financing and trust problem, not a hardware problem."

## Scoring

| Criterion | Weight | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impact | 25% | Share of customers metered within five years, and the reduction in estimated billing |
| Innovation | 25% | A new financing or cost-sharing model that removes upfront meter costs for customers |
| Sustainability | 25% | Cost recovery funded through energy purchases over five years; survives inflation and currency shocks; meters maintained and replaced |
| Feasibility | 15% | The financial model holds up under inflation and currency shocks |
| Scalability | 10% | Applies to utilities and mini-grids across several African countries |

## Constraints

- Test scenario: 15 million customers, 45% metered, heavy estimated
  billing, limited utility liquidity, high inflation, limited public
  funding.
- 100% metered within five years.
- Low-income customers pay no large upfront cost, and any meter charge
  stays a small share of the monthly bill.
- Utilities stay viable.
- Withstands currency swings and import-duty changes. The 2018 MAP scheme
  stalled when duty rose from 10% to 45%.
- Answers who pays, when, how costs are recovered, and how vulnerable
  customers are protected.
- Customers on free meters and on paid meters must not feel treated
  unfairly.

## Deliverables

Strategy, five-year financial model, deployment plan, digital prototype,
equity assessment.
